# Operating tips

Practical tips for getting more work done with less fuel, less wear and fewer warnings. Numbers
here (fuel rates, rpm, idle times) are **typical values from our demo fleet and assumptions we
chose, not Caterpillar specifications.** Safety always comes before speed or fuel savings.

## How can I save fuel on any machine?

- **Cut idle time.** High idle (above about 1200 rpm) burns 6–8 litres per hour doing nothing; low
  idle burns 2–4. If you wait more than about 10 minutes, shut down (once parked safely and, for a
  hot engine, after a 3–5 minute cool-down).
- **Use the right power mode.** Economy mode is enough for most loading, grading and backfilling.
  Use full power only for hard digging or steep climbs.
- **Plan short cycles.** Place trucks and piles to keep swing angles and travel distances short.
- **Avoid holding the relief valve.** Pushing against the end of a cylinder stroke or a stuck
  load wastes fuel and heats the hydraulic oil.
- **Keep the machine clean and serviced.** Blocked air filters and radiators raise fuel use and
  temperatures.

The app's weekly report shows your idle percentage and fuel per productive hour. The module
"Fuel-efficient operation" (TM-FUEL-01) and "Cutting idle time" (TM-IDLE-01) go into more detail.

## What idle percentage is good?

In our fleet, efficient operators idle about 6–10% of engine-on time, average operators 12–18%.
Above 25% in a week, the app recommends the "Cutting idle time" module. Some idle is unavoidable
(waiting for trucks, warm-up, cool-down), but long high-rpm idling usually is not.

## Why do smooth controls matter?

Jerky lever movements cause load spikes, pressure peaks and vibration. They:
- wear pins, bushings, hoses and tracks faster
- burn more fuel
- tire you out faster
- can unsettle the machine on a slope or with a raised load

The app counts harsh manoeuvres. Three or more in a week triggers the "Smooth controls" module
(TM-SMTH-01).

## How do I operate the controls smoothly?

- Start and stop every movement gradually: feather the levers in and out.
- Combine movements (boom up while swinging) instead of doing them one after another in jerks.
- Don't slam the bucket against the stops to shake off material; tilt gently instead.
- Keep engine speed steady rather than revving up and down.
- Look ahead and plan the next move, so you don't need sudden corrections.

## How should I work in rain?

1. **Slow down.** Stopping distances get longer and slopes lose grip. Speed limits on haul roads
   still apply, but drive below them when wet.
2. **Keep further from edges.** Trench walls and embankments get soft and can collapse.
3. **Watch the tilt reading.** Machines slide sideways more easily on wet clay.
4. **Use lights and wipers**, and expect people to be harder to see. The app automatically widens
   the proximity warning distances by 2 m in low visibility.
5. **Expect tasks to take longer.** The app's time estimates include rain and may re-plan the
   remaining tasks when heavy rain starts (more than about 2 mm per hour).
6. **Stop in lightning.** Stay in the cab of a closed machine away from high ground, or follow your
   site's lightning procedure.

## How should I work in dust or low visibility?

- Slow down and increase your following distance on haul roads.
- Switch on work lights and the beacon so others see you.
- Stop if you cannot see the edge of the work area, the truck you are loading or people around you.
- Rely on the camera screen for blind spots, but remember dust can hide people from the cameras too.
- Ask for water spraying on haul roads if dust stays heavy.

## Tips for excavator operators

- Position the truck so you swing 90° or less; every extra degree adds time and fuel.
- Dig with the bucket teeth at the right angle, and fill the bucket with one smooth curl instead of
  several small bites.
- Keep the tracks at right angles to a trench edge, with the travel motors at the back, away from
  the edge.
- Don't use the bucket or swing to push or drag heavy material sideways; it strains the swing system.
- On slopes: tracks up and down the slope, cut a level bench, and never swing a full bucket downhill.
- The instructor session "Excavator trenching technique" (TM-EXC-01) covers faster trenching.

## Tips for wheel loader operators

- Use the "V" loading pattern: short, tight cycles with the truck and pile at about 45° to each other.
- Enter the pile straight, with the bucket flat, and don't spin the tyres; wheel spin wears tyres
  and wastes fuel.
- Carry the bucket low while travelling, just high enough to clear the ground.
- Keep travel distances short; if the pile is far from the truck, ask for the layout to change.
- Check tyre pressure at the start of the shift.

## Tips for dozer operators

- Push with a full but not overloaded blade; if the tracks slip, you are carrying too much.
- Use slot dozing (pushing in the same path) to keep material in front of the blade.
- Push downhill when possible: gravity saves fuel.
- Grade with small blade corrections; big corrections make a wavy surface you have to fix later.
- Watch roll angle when pushing along a slope, and keep the blade low when travelling.

## Tips for articulated truck operators

- Stay within the haul road speed limits and speed-limited zones shown on the map.
- Use the retarder on downhill runs instead of riding the service brakes.
- Never turn sharply at speed; articulated trucks can tip when turning with a raised or uneven load.
- Dump only on level ground; raise the body slowly and never travel with it raised.
- Wait in the cab while being loaded, positioned so the loader operator can see you.
- The quiz "Haul road discipline" (TM-TRK-01) covers speed, spacing and right-of-way rules.

## How can I finish my tasks faster without cutting safety?

The app's time estimates show your usual pace for each task type. If you are regularly more than
20% slower than the estimate for a task type, the app suggests a technique module for it. Common
time savers: short cycles, good truck positioning, fewer repositioning moves, fewer idle gaps, and
asking early when access is blocked or trucks are late. Never save time by skipping the seatbelt,
working closer to edges, or swinging over people.

## What should I check at the start of the shift?

Read the handover brief from the previous shift in the app. Then walk around the machine:
- fluid leaks under the machine, damaged hoses
- tracks or tyres, bucket teeth and pins
- coolant, engine oil, hydraulic oil and fuel levels
- lights, mirrors, cameras clean
- seatbelt latches and holds
- no people or obstacles around the machine

Start the engine, let it warm up at low idle, check the gauges and the app for any warning before
you begin work.
