# Troubleshooting FAQ

Answers to common questions operators ask when a warning comes up in the cab. Every answer starts
with the **safe action first**. All limits quoted here (temperatures, pressures, voltages, angles,
times) are **assumptions we chose for this demo, not Caterpillar specifications**. On a real
machine, the limits come from that model's service manual.

The app never switches the machine off by itself. If a serious warning continues, it moves through
stages: warn → reduce power (derate, after about 2 minutes) → recommend safe shutdown (after about
5 minutes or if the value keeps rising) → notify the site manager (after about 7 minutes).

## Why is the engine overheating warning (COOLANT_HIGH) on?

**Safe action first:** stop heavy work, lower the attachment to the ground, move to level ground
and let the engine idle so the coolant can cool down. Never open the radiator cap while hot.

The warning comes on when engine coolant stays above 100 °C for 2 minutes (assumed limit). Common
causes are a radiator packed with dust or mud, low coolant, a loose fan belt, or long heavy work in
afternoon heat. If it clears after idling, check the radiator and coolant level once cool and report
it. If it comes back, call maintenance. This matches fault code E-110.

## The coolant temperature went critical (above 105 °C). What do I do?

**Safe action first:** lower any raised load, stop on level ground away from people, idle for 3–5
minutes, and then shut the engine down if the temperature does not fall. Do not keep working.

Above 105 °C (assumed limit) the engine can be damaged. The app will show step-by-step safe
shutdown instructions and, if the condition lasts, alert the site manager. Stay away from the
radiator cap and hoses; steam and hot coolant cause burns. Call maintenance before restarting.

## Why doesn't the system just switch off the machine when it overheats?

**Safe action first:** you stay in control: lower the load and get to level ground before any shutdown.

A sudden automatic stop can be more dangerous than the fault itself. The machine may be holding a
load in the air, travelling, or working on a slope. Stopping the engine can remove power steering,
hydraulic control or brake assist at the wrong moment. So the system warns, suggests reduced power,
then guides a safe shutdown, and escalates to the site manager if nothing changes.

## Can I switch off the engine immediately when it is very hot?

**Safe action first:** lower the attachment, then let the engine idle for 3–5 minutes before
switching off, unless there is fire, smoke, or an oil pressure warning.

Idling keeps coolant and oil moving and lets the turbocharger cool down. Switching off a very hot
engine straight from full load can cause heat damage. Exceptions: if you see fire or heavy smoke,
or the low oil pressure warning (E-215) is on, shut down right away once the machine is safely stopped.

## Why did the hydraulic pressure drop warning come on?

**Safe action first:** stop moving, lower the boom, bucket or blade slowly to the ground, and shut
down the engine. Keep people away from the machine.

This warning (HYD_PRESSURE_DROP) means hydraulic pressure suddenly fell to less than about 65% of
what it was a few minutes earlier while the machine was working and standing still (assumed rule).
The most likely cause is a leak: a burst hose, loose fitting or damaged cylinder seal. It is treated
as critical because a failing hydraulic line can drop a raised load.

## How do I look for a hydraulic leak safely?

**Safe action first:** attachment on the ground, engine off, pressure released, and **never use
your hand or fingers to feel for a leak**.

A pinhole leak can inject oil through skin, which needs emergency surgery. Look for wet or shiny
hoses, drips, puddles under the machine and oil mist. Use a piece of cardboard held at a distance to
find a spray. Report what you find, mark the area if oil is on the ground, and call maintenance. If
anyone is hit by a jet of oil, press SOS.

## The machine feels weak and slow and the hydraulic oil is hot. Is that related?

**Safe action first:** reduce the load, stop holding controls at the end of travel, and if the
hydraulic oil warning stays on, park on level ground with the attachment down and let it idle.

Hot hydraulic oil gets thinner and the machine loses force and speed. Our warning starts above
90 °C hydraulic oil temperature and is critical above 95 °C (assumed limits; normal is about
55–78 °C). Causes include a blocked oil cooler, low oil level, or holding the relief valve. If it
keeps happening, report it: a slow rise over days can be an early sign of a hydraulic failure (code E-365).

## Why is the low oil pressure warning on?

**Safe action first:** bring the machine to a safe stop with the attachment down, and shut the
engine off as soon as it is safe. Do not keep working.

The warning (OIL_PRESSURE_LOW, code E-215) fires when engine oil pressure is below 100 kPa while the
engine is above 1200 rpm (assumed limit). The engine may be low on oil, leaking oil, or the oil pump
may be failing. Running on without oil pressure can wreck the engine in minutes. Check the dipstick
once stopped and call maintenance before restarting.

## The oil pressure warning flashed once and went away. Can I keep working?

**Safe action first:** stop at a safe point, check the engine oil level and look for leaks before
carrying on.

A single short flash can be a sensor glitch, especially if it happened at idle or when the engine was
starting. But low oil pressure is too serious to guess. If the oil level is fine and the warning
does not return, report it at the end of the shift. If it comes on again, stop and call maintenance.

## Why is the battery or low voltage warning on?

**Safe action first:** finish the current movement safely and switch off electrical loads you
don't need. Avoid shutting down far from help, because the engine may not restart.

The warning (BATTERY_LOW, code E-410) comes on when system voltage stays below 24.0 V for 5 minutes
(assumed limit; normal running voltage is about 27–28.4 V). Common causes are a failing alternator,
a loose alternator belt, corroded battery terminals or an old battery. Tell maintenance before the
next shift.

## My display and lights are flickering. What should I do?

**Safe action first:** slow down and stop in a safe place. Don't drive at night or near people
without working lights and warning buzzers.

Flickering usually means the voltage is very low or a connection is loose. The machine's warnings
and camera display may stop working. Park with the attachment lowered, report it, and ask
maintenance to check the battery, terminals and alternator. Don't jump-start the machine unless you
are trained to.

## Why is the tilt warning (TIP_RISK) on?

**Safe action first:** stop moving, keep the bucket or blade low and close to the ground, and
don't turn across the slope. Wear your seatbelt.

The warning comes on when the machine's pitch (front to back) or roll (side to side) is more than
15° (assumed limit; critical above 25°). The machine is at risk of tipping, especially if you swing
a load or travel across the slope. Move slowly back to flatter ground, straight up or down the slope,
with the load low.

## What do I do if the machine starts to tip over?

**Safe action first:** stay in the seat with your seatbelt on, hold on firmly, brace your feet and
lean away from the direction of the fall. **Do not jump out.**

The rollover protective structure (ROPS) and seatbelt are designed to protect you inside the cab.
Operators who jump are often crushed by the machine. After the machine stops moving, switch off the
engine if you can, check yourself for injuries, and press SOS or call your supervisor before getting out.

## Can I swing a loaded bucket while working on a slope?

**Safe action first:** no. Keep the load low, and swing toward the uphill side only when necessary
and slowly.

Swinging a heavy load downhill moves the centre of gravity toward the low side and is a common cause
of excavator tip-overs. Where possible, cut a level bench to work from, keep tracks pointing up and
down the slope, and avoid working across it. If the tilt warning comes on, stop swinging and lower the load.

## Why is the seatbelt warning on?

**Safe action first:** stop the machine and fasten your seatbelt before moving again.

The warning (SEATBELT) comes on when the seatbelt is not fastened and the machine is moving (faster
than 0.5 km/h) or the engine is under more than 20% load, for more than 5 seconds (assumed rule).
After 30 seconds it becomes critical and your site manager is notified. Every violation is logged.

## The seatbelt warning is on but my belt is fastened. What now?

**Safe action first:** keep the belt on and check that it latches and holds when you pull it.

The switch may be faulty or dirty (this shows as code E-520, seatbelt switch fault). Unbuckle and
re-fasten once to see if the warning clears. If not, report it so maintenance can fix the switch.
Don't unbuckle to silence the alarm, and never operate if the belt itself doesn't latch.

## Why did I get an idle warning (EXCESS_IDLE)?

**Safe action first:** if you are waiting safely, lower the attachment and bring the engine down
to low idle, or shut down if the wait will be long.

The app flags idling at high rpm (above about 1200 rpm) for more than 10 minutes as info, and more
than 20 minutes as a warning (assumed rule). High idle burns 6–8 litres of fuel per hour for no work,
adds engine hours and wears the engine. Normal low idle is around 800–1000 rpm.

## When should I switch off instead of idling?

**Safe action first:** only shut down when the machine is parked on level ground with the
attachment lowered and it is safe to do so.

A good rule for our fleet: if you expect to wait more than about 5 minutes (for a truck, a
surveyor, or a blocked road), use low idle; if longer than about 10 minutes, shut down. Exceptions:
let a hot engine idle for 3–5 minutes before switching off, and keep it running if it may not
restart (low voltage warning).

## What do the warning stages (warn, derate, safe shutdown, escalated) mean?

**Safe action first:** whatever stage you see, the first step is the same: keep control, lower
any load, and move away from people and edges.

- **Warn:** banner and sound in the cab when a limit is crossed.
- **Derate:** after about 2 minutes the app recommends reduced power (economy mode) to lower heat and load.
- **Recommend safe shutdown:** after about 5 minutes, or if the value keeps rising, step-by-step
  instructions: lower the attachment, move to level ground, idle to cool, shut down.
- **Escalated:** after about 7 minutes or if ignored, the site manager is notified.
- **Resolved:** the reading has been back inside the limit for 2 minutes.

## The warning keeps turning on and off. Why?

**Safe action first:** treat it as a real warning until proven otherwise: reduce load and watch
the reading.

Our alerts only clear when the reading is 5% inside the limit for 2 minutes, so they should not
flicker. If a warning keeps returning, the value is sitting near the limit, which usually means a
real problem building up (a blocked radiator or weakening battery). Report it and ask maintenance
to check it before the next shift.
